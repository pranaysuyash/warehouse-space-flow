# Warehouse audit follow-through: temperature and workflow boundaries

Date: 2026-11-06. Scope: A14/F14 and the bounded P06 portions of A17/U06-3. This note records local scenario behavior and is not an operational WMS, physical-condition, food-safety or regulatory determination.

## Temperature range decision

`InboundPalletItem.storageTempRangeF` is treated as a declared commodity-acceptable interval, while `LocationSlot.temperatureRangeF` is treated as a declared interval of conditions the slot is expected to maintain. If both are declared and valid, the modeled slot interval must be contained within the commodity interval, including equal edges. Mere intersection can include temperatures outside the commodity's declared limits. Missing slot range for an explicitly constrained commodity is rejected; invalid, inverted and non-finite ranges are rejected. `temperatureRange.ts` is the common predicate used by putaway and goods-ledger compatibility helpers.

For legacy class-only scenarios, the default class range remains an indicative screen, not a commodity declaration. Missing commodity or slot values are called out as unknown; neither matching ranges nor a class label proves that a sensor measured conditions, that equipment maintained a guarantee, or that product temperature was safe. A future contract should distinguish equipment capability, setpoint, allowed excursion and measured history before making stronger decisions. Qualified commodity and site owners must validate accepted ranges and monitoring procedures.

Research inspected 2026-11-06: [FDA inspection guidance](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/inspection-guides/miscellaneous-food-products-vol2) emphasizes product-specific or label-derived temperatures and reliability of equipment/recording. [FDA HACCP guidance](https://www.fda.gov/food/hazard-analysis-critical-control-point-haccp/haccp-principles-application-guidelines) discusses monitoring critical limits and handling deviations. These sources motivate the distinction between declared limits and observed control; they do not prescribe this application's interval contract for all commodities or jurisdictions.

## Receiving, goods and outbound workflow trace

- `packages/shipment-flow/src/domain.mjs` owns the local-scenario `deliver` transition. It validates original handling-unit IDs, barcodes and accepted slot codes, then stores receiver, receipt time and units in the revisioned shipment. It explicitly describes receipt as manually entered scenario evidence.
- `src/engine/shipmentAdapter.ts` projects a delivered shipment into a scenario facility with occupied unit/slot assignments. Its Cargo Fit translator synthesizes SKU and customer labels. `Shipment.input.units` has no explicit stock-owner, SKU, item quantity, lot, condition or outbound release contract.
- `GoodsLedger` is a distinct in-memory demonstration mounted by `GoodsLedgerModal`, with no shared persistence or synchronization. It now requires explicit customer, SKU, unit, location and condition, rejects non-finite/invalid quantities, mismatched units and duplicate movement IDs, checks available good stock by selected slot, and omits exhausted slots from current balances. A read-only view of `Shipment.delivery` is displayed from the shared shipment record, without copying or interpreting it as stock; Cargo Fit's synthetic SKU/customer labels are not prefilled as ledger facts.
- `CrossDockModal` previously supplied neither inbound pallets nor outbound orders and called a no-op action “Dispatch”. It now exposes optional supplied inputs, labels the empty case, and disables its review action without matches and a callback. The `crossDockMatching.ts` and separate `crossDockMatcher.ts` helpers remain preserved; their assumptions and partial-order/pallet handling do not establish a complete allocation or execution contract. The result is a proposal, not a stock movement.
- `WavePickingModal` uses `SAMPLE_OUTBOUND_WAVE_ITEMS`; it now labels this as a sample and its close action as a close, not a release. Its route and lot logic does not establish a real pick, FEFO compliance, observed inventory or custody.
- `@trucking/storage-core` contains shared foundational types and is not currently an authoritative stock ledger. P09 owns landlord/lease data, not a verified P06 goods ledger. P06 local reservation/event helpers are separate from the shared scenario service. They are not an atomic multi-tenant inventory database.

The smallest useful supported receiving flow is therefore a read-only projection of the existing scenario receipt, implemented here. A durable receive-to-stock-to-outbound flow needs an owner-approved canonical inventory authority; facility and tenant identities; handling-unit/SKU/quantity/unit/lot/condition contracts; idempotent, revisioned and atomic receipts, allocations, picking and dispatch; authenticated actors and observation provenance; and explicit offline conflict and reversal rules. Do not create a second writable stock store or fabricate those fields to bridge this gap. A future decision is needed on how the shared shipment scenario record relates to the warehouse inventory authority, and which customer/property information may cross P06/P09 boundaries.

## Evidence and remaining validation

Before the corrective changes, focused S2 regressions showed that an unknown temperature range had no warning, the cross-dock no-op looked like dispatch, the sample wave looked releasable, the ledger invented customer defaults, a non-finite quantity was accepted, and a depleted slot remained in current stock locations. Focused tests now cover those paths, range containment and boundaries, unit and slot mismatches, quarantined stock, and rendering a shared scenario receipt without a stock claim. Exact product test and typecheck results are reported in the suite audit register. These are T2/S2 for the exercised source and server-rendered states, not browser, scanner, equipment, production, or real-user evidence. Existing scenario helpers, historical work, and pre-existing dirty files are retained.

## 3D facility workspace follow-through

The active facility is the same `displayedFacility` supplied by the 2D planner, or the existing connected shipment projection. `facilityScene.ts` maps the facility's declared 2D zone/location coordinates, location type, level and status to the existing SVG projection. Location selection shows only that slot's own code, status, occupant identifier, declared weight and slot rating. Invalid or missing location rectangles are excluded with a count, not given sample coordinates. Extents are normalized for viewing and vertical heights are schematic: neither surveyed 3D measurements nor physical occupancy is established by the display. Equipment positions and ABC velocity classes are not recorded by this workspace, so their 3D controls indicate missing data rather than displaying the previous invented forklift and classifications. The old standalone scene-construction code and its tests remain preserved; it is no longer shown as the current facility.

`tests/facilityScene.test.ts` checks workspace identities, statuses, coordinate changes, layer filtering, invalid and overflowing geometry and initial viewport bounds; `tests/evidenceUi.test.tsx` checks rendered slot information and absence of the old fixed SKU and beam claim. Full `npm test` passed 25 files/134 tests and `npm run typecheck` passed, T2/S2 for those behaviors. The local IAB was unavailable in this agent session, so there is no current before/after visual capture, pointer/touch test, connected-session browser proof, real facility survey or deployed verification. Those remain required before visual/operational acceptance.
